you and someone else find a laptop bag near a terminal after midnight.

it's raining hard, rides are barely moving, and whoever owns the laptop probably hasn't been gone for long.

the laptop is unlocked.

that's pretty much all you know.

figure out who it belongs to.

after that... just explore.

how to play

there isn't really one correct order.

open apps.

read messages.

check files.

look through photos.

compare timestamps.

go back to things you've already seen once you know more.

some stuff matters immediately.

some stuff won't make sense until way later.

and some stuff is genuinely just random shit somebody had on their laptop.

pay attention to everything.

but don't assume everything means something.

investigation

later in the game, you'll get tools that let you organize what you've found.

you can mark evidence as:

confirmed

likely

possible

speculation

these are your own conclusions, not the game's answer.

something appearing on the laptop does not automatically prove who created it.

someone remembering something does not automatically mean they remembered it perfectly.

two things happening close together does not automatically mean they're connected.

and the same name appearing twice doesn't always mean it's the same thing.

revisit stuff

if something feels useless right now, you can mark it to come back to later.

honestly, do that.

a boring detail can feel completely different once you know what happened before and after it.

sometimes the game isn't giving you a new clue.

sometimes it's giving you a reason to look at an old one differently.

the laptop is offline

most of what you see at first is cached or saved locally.

that matters.

you won't have proper live access immediately.

if you eventually manage to reconnect it, remember:

something received later isn't necessarily something that existed when the laptop was abandoned.

photos

don't expect magic enhancement.

if a photo is blurry, it's blurry.

if rain covers something, rain covers it.

zooming six pixels isn't going to suddenly identify somebody.

sometimes what's wrong with a photo isn't what it shows.

sometimes it's how it was taken.

messages

people lie.

people exaggerate.

people forget.

people describe things differently.

people also say stuff like:

i'm going home

without literally meaning:

i am currently inside my house.

don't treat every contradiction like a confession.

timestamps

timestamps are probably the closest thing you have to something objective.

even then, be careful.

a file being opened proves:

a file was opened.

it does not automatically prove:

who opened it.

a laptop lid closing proves:

the laptop lid closed.

nothing more.

one important thing

when you first get access to the laptop...

look at what was already there before you start opening shit.

you only get a first look once.

another important thing

if you swear you read something differently earlier...

maybe you did.

before deciding the game is fucking with you, check whether there's a boring technical explanation first.

computers save drafts.

apps cache older copies.

files get restored.

memory is also kinda shit.
